# Cinematic Example Matrix

Use this matrix for multi-variant generation, random stress tests, multi-shot sequences, and unfamiliar scene families. It distills recurring lessons from a broad cinematic triptych example set across domestic, ritual, sports, speculative, animated, historical, and memory-driven subjects.

This is not a prompt library. Do not copy subjects, props, character arrangements, locations, color combinations, or story clues from it. Use only the diagnostic questions and variation axes.

## 1. Scene-family diagnostics

| Scene family | Pressure question | Useful visual-traffic source | Common failure to prevent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic / family / interior | Who belongs, waits, knows, leaves, or is excluded, and how does the room show it? | table edges, doorways, seating gaps, windows, corridors, practical lamps | dialogue-blocking television frame, decorative nostalgia, abandoned-object cliché |
| Institutional / civic / public | Which rule, queue, ritual, witness position, or controlled route limits the subject? | rails, counters, tiled lanes, crowd gaps, signs as geometry, surveillance barriers | generic dystopia, meaningless extras, global fluorescent grime |
| Ritual / historical / Eastern space | What action, refusal, rank, threshold, or alignment makes the symmetry meaningful? | axial floors, steps, screens, ceremonial spacing, fabric or shadow direction | costume-poster gloss, tourism image, fantasy glow, decorative crowds |
| Sport / speed / physical pressure | What physical decision or near-miss is legible without heroic advertising? | ball/vehicle trajectory, fence, spray, body lean, lane, teammate wall, compressed layers | fake speed trails, glowing machinery, showroom polish, identical action angles |
| Speculative / science-fiction environment | What ordinary rule, labor, route, institution, or survival condition makes the world believable? | transit line, access gate, maintenance path, queue, pressure boundary, scale contrast | neon city default, giant spectacle without function, game key art |
| Water / sea / underwater | What controls visibility, access, pressure, reflection, or departure? | horizon, glass, caustics, hull shadow, waterline, bubbles only when motivated | postcard beauty, global blue filter, decorative caustics, too many nautical details |
| Creature / anthropomorphic / animation | What original social rule and physical behavior makes the subject distinct without copying an IP? | stage edge, habitat architecture, body scale, gesture, handmade material or animation staging | recognizable franchise structure, mascot posing, generic cute city, borrowed character roles |
| Absurd / county / social realism | What mundane inconsistency is treated seriously rather than staged as a joke? | static observation, weak practical signs, waiting geometry, ordinary object contrast | random grime, horror lighting, dreamcore gloss, comedy mugging |
| Memory / departure / aftermath | What changed access, distance, relation, or routine remains after the decisive moment? | reflection, empty path, continued activity, altered spacing, mediated view, off-screen direction | sentimental object close-up, empty-room formula, heavy vignette, unexplained blur |
| Landscape / architecture / settlement | What route, barrier, scale relation, or environmental dominance organizes the frame? | roads, ridges, courtyards, walls, watercourses, light paths, overlapping planes | empty matte painting, uniformly detailed map, tiny figures added without need |

## 2. Variation axes

When generating several options, vary decisions rather than nouns. Change at least three axes across the set:

- relationship pressure: observed, confined, separated, unequal, unstable, aftermath;
- audience position: participant, outsider, wrong side of barrier, elevated observer, low vulnerable witness, mediated/reflected view;
- visual entry: body edge, route, architecture, contrast patch, motion line, reflection, shadow, negative space;
- visual interruption: occlusion, scale jump, light boundary, crossing movement, compression, focus transition;
- final unresolved area: edge action, blocked destination, reflection, off-screen gaze, continuing process, environmental consequence;
- color logic: high-key separation, earth/oxidized accent, warm practical versus cool ambient, near-monochrome material contrast, natural high-chroma accent, medium-specific color loss;
- capture behavior: stable digital narrative, 35mm softness, 16mm immediacy, mediated low fidelity, long-lens compression, motivated wide witness view;
- information function: geography, relationship, rule, discovery, impact, reaction, continuation, aftermath.

Do not vary everything at once. Preserve the user's world, content constraints, and selected style anchor.

## 3. Three-frame stress test

For a three-frame request, privately confirm:

1. Frame one establishes a pressure, access condition, or spatial rule rather than merely showing a wide shot.
2. Frame two changes knowledge, relationship, action, or physical pressure rather than merely moving closer.
3. Frame three shows consequence, continued action, altered relation, collective response, or environment state rather than automatically isolating a leftover object.
4. Each frame has a different visual-traffic path.
5. The shared color thesis and capture substrate remain recognizable.
6. Only one frame uses a special lens/light event when justified.
7. Shuffling the frames weakens the sequence.

## 4. Anti-template audit

Reject a batch when:

- every option uses the same human-view wide shot, foreground blur, center corridor, low-angle monument, eye close-up, or doorway frame;
- every serious scene becomes rainy, dark, blue-gray, teal-orange, neon, or low saturation;
- every final frame is an empty room, dropped object, abandoned chair, ticket, letter, or reflection;
- speculative scenes rely on particles, glowing interfaces, giant structures, or impossible rim light instead of functional world rules;
- historical and ritual scenes rely on costumes and symmetrical beauty without a visible action or power relation;
- sports scenes become brand advertising instead of physical timing and consequence;
- animation or anthropomorphic scenes inherit recognizable IP roles, city systems, silhouettes, or character groupings;
- prompt density grows with every variant until focal hierarchy and physical plausibility collapse.
