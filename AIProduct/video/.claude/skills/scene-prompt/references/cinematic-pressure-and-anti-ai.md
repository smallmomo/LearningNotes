# Cinematic Pressure And Anti-AI Calibration

Read this reference for realistic or cinematic stills, narrative subjects, anti-AI correction, optical-texture decisions, special lens/light treatments, or multi-shot/triptych requests. Apply only the parts supported by the user's brief and selected style anchor.

## 1. Relationship and spatial pressure

Build the frame from a relationship rather than a composition preset.

- **Observed:** glass, doorway, crowd edge, monitor, reflection, surveillance distance, or another physically justified barrier changes who can see whom.
- **Confined:** architecture, furniture, terrain, queue, corridor, vehicle interior, institutional layout, or weather limits movement or access.
- **Separated:** negative space, a route, elevation, water, a table, a threshold, or scale expresses emotional or functional distance.
- **Unequal:** one subject is compressed by a wall, stage, monument, slope, crowd, machine, or dominant environmental mass.
- **Unstable:** excessive headroom, edge pressure, partial occlusion, low horizon, tilted route, or off-center balance is motivated by danger, doubt, surveillance, or loss of control.
- **Aftermath:** the decisive action is over, but changed spacing, posture, access, light, or environmental operation makes its consequence visible.

For environment-led scenes, treat a road, entrance, settlement, vessel, structure, creature, or light source as the active subject. Do not invent characters merely to manufacture drama.

## 2. Captured-frame discipline

Make the scene feel observed through optical, production, and timing limits rather than assembled to display nouns.

- Keep one primary story clue or focal anchor and no more than two supporting clues.
- Let ordinary, soft, dark, empty, partially hidden, or visually quiet areas remain.
- Prefer one decisive instant: waiting, entering, refusing, missing, turning, departing, discovering, being blocked, or another brief-supported state.
- Avoid equal sharpness, equal brightness, decorative debris, global wet gloss, smoke in every layer, over-designed props, and exhaustive micro-detail.
- Replace generic prestige words with visible facts. Avoid using `epic`, `masterpiece`, `beautiful`, `highly detailed`, `intricate`, `dramatic lighting`, or `volumetric` as substitutes for decisions.

## 3. Capture substrate and texture

Choose one coherent capture or rendering substrate when texture matters. Do not stack incompatible media signatures.

When naming a specific film stock, format, or print-film emulation, read `film-stock-selection.md` and apply its physical-film versus digital-emulation rules.

- **Grounded digital cinema:** restrained noise, natural highlight rolloff, readable shadow separation, controlled local sharpness.
- **35mm narrative capture:** medium-low microcontrast, gentle highlight density, subtle uneven grain, limited local softness.
- **16mm documentary or broadcast transfer:** softer detail, coarser natural grain, slight color bleed, less stable exposure.
- **Early digital or MiniDV:** practical-light immediacy, limited dynamic range, mild edge hardness, small-sensor depth behavior.
- **Mediated/rephotographed view:** monitor texture, geometry or glare, black compression, scan softness, or reflection only when the camera is physically looking through a device or surface.
- **Long-lens compression:** restrained focus depth, layered atmospheric compression, window/heat/rain distortion when physically present.
- **Wide witness view:** optical distortion only when the camera is trapped inside a doorway, vehicle, booth, crowd, narrow room, device, or similarly motivated position.

The capture choice must support the scene's time, light, camera position, and style anchor. A clean PBR, illustration, animation, architectural visualization, or product-render brief should use its own coherent rendering logic instead of forced film defects.

## 4. Controlled imperfection

Use selective imperfection. Choose at most one dominant family unless the user explicitly requests a layered degradation treatment:

- dust or dry abrasion;
- rain and localized wet reflection;
- smoke with weak practical lamps;
- fluorescent institutional wear;
- broadcast/video noise;
- fogged glass or condensation;
- sun-faded surfaces and chipped coating;
- motion blur, focus hesitation, or partial occlusion caused by the camera position.

Imperfection must have a physical source and a spatial location. Do not make every surface dirty, every highlight bloom, every edge chromatically separated, or every plane equally grainy.

## 5. Special lens and light inserts

Use a special lens or light event only when it changes access, pressure, knowledge, or attention.

- Justify wide/fisheye distortion through a confined witness position.
- Derive caustics from water, glass, aquarium surfaces, a flooded floor, or moving reflection; keep them local.
- Derive strong shadows from blinds, railings, fan blades, projector gates, windows, branches, headlights, signage, or practical fixtures.
- Preserve foreground, middle ground, background, and a readable focal anchor; an effect cannot replace staging.
- Keep the existing color thesis. A special light may add one accent but cannot introduce an unrelated palette.

Reject random fisheye exteriors, global rainbow caustics, unjustified horror shadows, neon/laser spectacle, artificial rim light, and commercial beauty lighting when they do not belong to the brief.

## 6. Multi-frame rhythm

For two or more connected frames:

- Generate frames separately and assemble outside the image model when a strip or triptych is required.
- Keep one world, capture substrate, material logic, and color thesis unless a motivated transition is part of the story.
- Vary audience position, access, focal distance, information, or consequence rather than merely changing focal length.
- Allow one visually quiet or transitional frame when it makes the surrounding frames more believable.
- In a three-frame sequence, use at most one special lens/light insert; keep two frames optically stable.
- Make order consequential. A valid sequence loses meaning when shuffled.
- Do not default the last frame to an abandoned object or empty room. It may end on a changed relationship, continued public activity, physical recovery, altered access, collective reaction, or environmental consequence.

When the user specifies unequal panel weights or exact frame ratios, preserve them. Otherwise avoid inventing a collage layout or aspect ratio.

## 7. Rejection conditions

Reject and rebuild when any of these materially affects the result:

- all areas are equally expensive, complete, sharp, textured, or illuminated;
- the scene resembles advertising, a game key visual, generic concept art, or glossy AI rendering against the requested realistic anchor;
- dirt, haze, bloom, grain, wetness, particles, or color fringing is global and decorative;
- the camera position, distortion, reflection, or light event has no physical reason;
- a multi-frame sequence remains equally readable after shuffling;
- every frame repeats the same pressure, visual-traffic path, scale, or composition;
- the color system is a generic filter rather than a physical relationship among surfaces, light, weather, and medium.
